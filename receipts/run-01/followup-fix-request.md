Order them by information value, not by closability — those give opposite answers here, and your landman's "three in a week" is closability-ordering.

First errand, and it's one trip: column (e) on Doc. 2009-04471, the complete 1961 deed at Vol 212 Pg 88, and the full legal description in the 1981 Sandridge lease. All three are instrument pulls at the same clerk's office or abstract plant. Hours, not days.

That single errand does three things:

* Column (e) either kills the tax lien outright or tells you it may be the real decider — a $118,000 lien plus seventeen years of interest against a $310,000 acquisition would displace the reservation. You want that answer on day 3, not day 40.
* The full 1961 deed is the only thing that can move the red. If the four corners fix the fraction internally, the flag goes orange and the restructure math changes.
* The 1981 lease description is the input to everything after it. You can't run the production search without knowing which 2,880 acres to run.

Second: the RRC production search across the entire leased premises, plus any recorded pooling designation, extension, ratification, or partial release. Two to three days. This is what sizes the reservation — floating versus fixed is economically identical under a 1/8 royalty and diverges by roughly a third of your royalty position under a modern one. No restructured number should be set before this comes back.

Last: entity authority and the name variance. Both will clear. Neither tells you anything you don't already know. They're schedule items — the managing member in Amarillo isn't going anywhere in three weeks, and running landman hours on them before the first errand comes back is spending on a deal you may not be doing.

One dependency worth carrying forward: when you search the federal tax lien index for a refiling, the name-variance facts are what make that search complete. Run both "Marvin J. Ostroff" and "M. Jay Ostroff" before you conclude nothing was refiled.

That's the ordering of what to learn. What to do with each answer — close, reprice, or walk — stays yours.
