# Title Defect Failure Modes

A taxonomy of title defects organized by type. Use this as a checklist during Step 3 (Flag Every Defect) of the diagnostic process.

---

## 1. Chain of Title Gaps

A break in the sequence of conveyances from sovereign to current owner.

| Failure Mode | Description |
|---|---|
| **Grantor-grantee mismatch** | Grantor in a deed was never the grantee in a prior instrument. The chain skips a link. |
| **Missing conveyance** | A transfer of ownership occurred (by sale, gift, or operation of law) but no instrument appears of record to document it. |
| **Wild deed** | A recorded deed from a grantor who does not appear in the chain of title. Cannot be connected to the chain without additional evidence. |
| **Stranger to title** | Someone who never held record title executed a conveyance. May be a color-of-title issue or a simple error. |
| **Unrecorded conveyance** | A valid deed was executed and delivered but never recorded. Affects constructive notice and priority. |

---

## 2. Heirship and Probate Defects

Problems arising from the death of a title holder.

| Failure Mode | Description |
|---|---|
| **Intestate death, no determination** | Title holder died without a will and no affidavit of heirship or judicial determination of heirship appears of record. Heirs and their fractional shares are unknown. |
| **Probate not filed or not completed** | Title holder died with a will, but the will was never probated, or probate proceedings were opened and never concluded. |
| **Will not admitted to probate in county of land** | Will probated in a different county. Authenticated copy may not have been filed in the county where the land is located. |
| **Heirship fragmentation** | An intestate death (often generations back) has been compounded by subsequent deaths, creating a large and growing class of fractional interest holders. |
| **Unknown or missing heirs** | One or more heirs identified in an heirship determination cannot be located. Their consent is needed for a transaction. |
| **Defective affidavit of heirship** | Affidavit is missing required elements (two disinterested witnesses, proper recitations) or contains factual errors about family relationships. |
| **Community property complications** | Deceased was married, and the community property interest of the surviving spouse was not properly addressed in the conveyance chain. |

---

## 3. Instrument Defects

Problems with the documents themselves.

| Failure Mode | Description |
|---|---|
| **Missing signature** | One or more required parties did not sign the instrument (e.g., both spouses on homestead property). |
| **Defective acknowledgment** | Notary certificate is missing, incomplete, expired, or from wrong jurisdiction. |
| **Missing venue on acknowledgment** | Notary certificate does not state the county and state where the acknowledgment was taken. |
| **Unauthorized signer** | Someone signed on behalf of another without recorded power of attorney, corporate resolution, or other authority. |
| **Entity authority gap** | Corporation, LLC, or trust executed a deed, but no evidence of authority (resolution, certificate of formation, trust instrument) appears of record. |
| **Undelivered instrument** | Evidence suggests a deed was signed but never legally delivered (e.g., remained in grantor's possession, recorded after grantor's death by someone other than grantee). |

---

## 4. Legal Description Defects

Problems with the property description in instruments.

| Failure Mode | Description |
|---|---|
| **Ambiguous description** | Description does not uniquely identify the property. Could refer to more than one tract. |
| **Conflicting descriptions** | Metes and bounds calls conflict with each other, or metes and bounds conflict with a referenced survey or plat. |
| **Wrong survey reference** | Deed references wrong survey name, abstract number, or block number. |
| **Acreage discrepancy** | Stated acreage differs significantly from computed acreage of the described tract. May indicate the description captures more or less land than intended. |
| **Missing exhibit** | Deed references an attached exhibit for the legal description ("as described in Exhibit A attached hereto") but the exhibit was not recorded. |
| **Overlapping surveys** | The described tract falls within an area where two or more original surveys overlap, creating competing claims. |

---

## 5. Encumbrance Defects

Outstanding interests, liens, or restrictions that burden the title.

| Failure Mode | Description |
|---|---|
| **Unreleased deed of trust/mortgage** | A recorded lien instrument was never released, creating an apparent encumbrance regardless of whether the debt was actually satisfied. |
| **Outstanding judgment lien** | An abstract of judgment filed against a party in the chain has not been released or satisfied of record. |
| **Federal tax lien** | IRS filed a notice of federal tax lien against a party in the chain. Survives conveyance for the statutory notice period. |
| **Mechanic's or materialman's lien** | Lien filed for unpaid construction work. May have priority that relates back to the start of the project. |
| **Lis pendens** | Notice of pending lawsuit affecting title to the property is filed of record. |
| **Outstanding mineral lease** | An oil and gas lease burdening the minerals has not expired or been released and may conflict with the proposed transaction. |

---

## 6. Reservation and Exception Defects

Problems with language retaining interests upon conveyance.

| Failure Mode | Description |
|---|---|
| **Mineral vs. royalty ambiguity** | Reservation uses "royalty" language but may have intended to reserve a mineral interest (or vice versa). The distinction determines executive rights, bonus, delay rental, and lease participation. |
| **Fraction-of-fraction ambiguity** | Reservation of "1/2 of grantor's 1/2 interest" — unclear whether the result is 1/4 of the whole or 1/2 of the whole. The "two-grant" vs. "restated fraction" problem. |
| **Reservation vs. exception confusion** | Instrument uses "reserve" and "except" interchangeably or inconsistently. These are different legal concepts (reservation creates a new interest in the grantor; exception withholds an existing interest from the grant). |
| **Term interest expiration** | A reserved interest was for a term of years or "so long as" a condition exists. Whether the interest has expired or reverted is unclear or disputed. |
| **Subsequent conveyance of reserved interest** | The reserved interest was conveyed by a separate instrument, but the chain of that interest is incomplete or conflicts with the surface chain. |
| **"Subject to" with unclear scope** | Conveyance is "subject to" existing reservations, leases, or encumbrances, but does not identify them specifically. Scope of the carve-out is indeterminate. |

---

## 7. Recording Defects

Problems with how instruments were placed in the public record.

| Failure Mode | Description |
|---|---|
| **Late recording** | Instrument was recorded substantially after execution, creating a gap in which intervening bona fide purchasers may have acquired superior rights. |
| **Wrong county** | Instrument was recorded in a county other than where the land is situated. Constructive notice may be ineffective. |
| **Mis-indexed** | Instrument was recorded but indexed under the wrong name, wrong tract, or wrong book. Effectively invisible to a title search. |
| **Wrong book** | Instrument was recorded in the wrong record series (e.g., a deed of trust recorded in the deed records instead of the deed of trust records). |
