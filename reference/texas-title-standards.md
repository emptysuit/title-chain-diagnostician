# Texas Title Examination Standards

Key Texas-specific rules, statutory presumptions, and case law relevant to title chain diagnosis. Reference this when a conclusion depends on a Texas-specific rule.

**This file mostly serves factor (d)** — *does the law give a clear answer?* (`rules.md` Step 5). Where a standard or statute settles the question, the cure is priceable and the flag can be set with confidence. Where the law is genuinely unsettled — the mineral-vs-royalty constructions being the standing example — no amount of record work resolves it, a court or a stipulation does, and the flag goes red on that basis alone.

Texas also moves flags *toward yellow* more than most states, because the affidavit of heirship procedure makes curable some heirship defects that would require a judicial proceeding elsewhere. Do not carry that assumption into another jurisdiction.

This is not a comprehensive title manual. It covers the standards most commonly implicated in title chain failures.

---

## Chain of Title Standards

### Recording and Constructive Notice
- Texas is a "race-notice" state. A subsequent bona fide purchaser for value without notice who records first takes priority over a prior unrecorded conveyance. (Tex. Prop. Code Sec. 13.001)
- Recording creates constructive notice only when the instrument is filed in the correct county and properly indexed. Mis-indexed or wrong-county instruments may fail to provide constructive notice.

### Statute of Limitations on Title Claims
- **Adverse possession (3-year):** When claimant holds under title or color of title. (Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code Sec. 16.024)
- **Adverse possession (5-year):** When claimant has cultivated, used, or enjoyed under a recorded deed and paid taxes. (Sec. 16.025)
- **Adverse possession (10-year):** General limitations for peaceable and adverse possession. (Sec. 16.026)
- **Adverse possession (25-year):** Applies regardless of disability. Creates a strong presumption. (Sec. 16.027-16.028)
- **Dormant mineral interests:** Under Tex. Nat. Res. Code Ch. 75, a mineral interest is presumed abandoned if unused for 25+ years. The surface owner may reclaim it through a statutory notice process.

### Ancient Documents
- Texas courts give a presumption of validity to instruments recorded for a long period (generally 30+ years) even if there are defects in execution. The older the instrument and the longer the chain has relied on it, the stronger the presumption.

## Heirship Standards

### Affidavit of Heirship (Tex. Est. Code Sec. 203.001 et seq.)
- An affidavit of heirship is a sworn statement by a disinterested person identifying the heirs of a decedent and their shares.
- Must be signed by two disinterested witnesses who knew the decedent and the family.
- When filed of record for 5+ years, creates a prima facie evidentiary standard on matters of heirship, identity, and marital history. (Tex. Est. Code Sec. 203.001)
- An affidavit of heirship is commonly accepted by title companies in Texas as sufficient evidence of heirship for curative purposes, but it is NOT a judicial determination and can be challenged.

### Judicial Determination of Heirship (Tex. Est. Code Sec. 202.001 et seq.)
- A court proceeding that conclusively determines the heirs of a decedent and their respective shares.
- Required when heirship is contested, complex, or involves unknown or missing heirs.
- A judicial determination is binding on all parties including unknown heirs if proper service by publication was made.

### Community Property Presumption
- Property acquired during marriage is presumed to be community property. (Tex. Fam. Code Sec. 3.003)
- Upon the death of a spouse, the surviving spouse owns their 1/2 community property interest outright. The decedent's 1/2 passes under the will or by intestate succession.
- Failure to account for the community property interest of a surviving spouse is a common chain defect.

### Intestate Succession (Tex. Est. Code Sec. 201.001 et seq.)
- Real property passes to the decedent's heirs at law according to the statutory scheme.
- Separate vs. community property affects the distribution (surviving spouse takes different shares depending on whether property is separate or community, and whether there are children).

## Mineral Interest Standards

### Mineral Estate as Dominant Estate
- In Texas, the mineral estate is the dominant estate. The mineral owner has the right to use as much of the surface as is reasonably necessary to explore, develop, and produce minerals. (Tex. Nat. Res. Code; Getty Oil Co. v. Jones)

### The Five Attributes of a Mineral Interest
Texas courts recognize five attributes of mineral ownership:
1. **Right to develop** (executive right to lease)
2. **Right to lease** (execute oil and gas leases)
3. **Right to receive bonus** (signing bonus for a lease)
4. **Right to receive delay rentals** (payments to maintain a lease during the primary term)
5. **Right to receive royalty** (share of production)

These attributes can be severed and owned separately. When diagnosing, determine which attributes are at issue and who holds them.

### Mineral vs. Royalty Interest
- A **mineral interest** includes all five attributes. The owner can lease, receive bonus, delay rentals, and royalty.
- A **royalty interest** includes only the right to receive royalty (a share of production). No executive rights, no bonus, no delay rentals.
- A **nonparticipating royalty interest (NPRI)** is a royalty interest carved out of the mineral estate. The NPRI owner receives their royalty share but has no right to lease or participate in lease benefits.

### The Estate Misconception Doctrine
- When a grantor uses "royalty" language but the surrounding circumstances suggest they intended to reserve a "mineral" interest, Texas courts may construe the reservation as a mineral interest despite the royalty language.
- This is the single most common source of ambiguity in Texas mineral title chains.
- Key cases: Luckel v. White, French v. Chevron U.S.A., Hysaw v. Dawkins, Garza v. Garza.
- The result is unpredictable and highly fact-dependent. When this ambiguity appears in a chain, the executive rights issue is a deal-killer unless resolved by stipulation or declaratory judgment.

### The Two-Grant Doctrine and Fraction Problems
- A conveyance of "an undivided 1/2 of my undivided 1/2 mineral interest" creates an ambiguity: is the result a 1/4 mineral interest (mathematical product) or a 1/2 mineral interest (the second fraction restates the first)?
- Under the "two-grant" approach, the grantor conveys 1/4 (1/2 of their 1/2).
- Under the "restated fraction" approach, the fractions are not multiplied; the second fraction clarifies the first, and the grantee receives 1/2.
- Texas courts have gone both ways. Interpretation depends on the specific language and context.

## Deed of Trust / Lien Standards

### Statute of Limitations on Liens
- A real property lien becomes unenforceable after the 4-year statute of limitations on the underlying debt runs. (Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code Sec. 16.035)
- However, the lien remains of record and clouds title until a release is filed, the statute of limitations is pleaded, or a court orders its removal.
- A title company may insure over a stale lien but typically requires an affidavit or indemnity.

### Federal Tax Liens
- An IRS tax lien attaches to all property of the taxpayer, including real property.
- A federal tax lien filed of record is effective against subsequent purchasers and lien creditors for the statutory period.
- Release or subordination requires IRS action. This is outside the control of the parties to the transaction and can take significant time.

## Homestead Standards

### Constitutional Homestead Protection (Tex. Const. Art. XVI, Sec. 50)
- Texas homestead property cannot be encumbered except for specific constitutional exceptions (purchase money, taxes, home improvement, home equity, etc.).
- A lien on homestead property that does not fall within one of the constitutional exceptions is void.
- Both spouses must join in a conveyance or encumbrance of homestead property regardless of how title is held.
- Homestead character is determined by use and intent, not by how the deed reads.
